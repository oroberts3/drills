Traffic Lights WarmupDribbling5–8 min
⌄
Setup

20×20-ish grid. Every player has a ball.

How to play
  • Green = dribble.
  • Yellow = slow, tiny touches.
  • Red = stop the ball.
  • Add purple = turn around, blue = 3 toe taps, etc.
Coaching cues

Head up when possible. Keep the ball close in traffic; use a bigger touch when there is open grass.

U6Keep commands simple and silly. No elimination.
U7Add right-foot/left-foot rounds, pullbacks, and changes of direction.
Coach SaysWarmupBall control5–8 min
⌄
Setup

Everyone has a ball inside a grid.

How to play

Simon Says with a soccer ball: stop it, pull it back, turn, toe taps, put a knee/elbow on it, dribble around a cone, find new space.

Coaching cues

Keep them moving. Use commands to sneak in technique without stopping for long explanations.

U6Favor fun body-part commands and basic stops/starts.
U7Add sole rolls, inside/outside touches, and weak-foot challenges.
Sharks & MinnowsDribblingVs8–10 min
⌄
Setup

Players with balls on one end line. 1–2 sharks begin in the middle.

How to play

Minnows dribble to the opposite end without having their ball knocked out. If a ball goes out, either re-enter immediately or become a shark.

Coaching cues

“Can you see the shark and the ball?” “Where is the open space?” Change speed after getting around pressure.

U6Use immediate re-entry so nobody stands around.
U7Encourage shielding, fakes, and deliberate changes of direction.
GatesDribblingSolo8–10 min
⌄
Setup

Scatter 8–12 cone gates around the field. Every player has a ball.

How to play
  • Score 1 point for dribbling through a gate.
  • Try for as many as possible in 45 seconds.
  • Cannot use the same gate twice in a row.
Progressions

Right foot through one, left through the next; turn after each gate; pair up and pass through gates.

U6Mostly free dribbling and counting gates.
U7Turn it into partner passing or require scanning for an unused gate.
Freeze TagWarmupTeam8 min
⌄
Setup

Everyone dribbles in a grid. Choose 1–2 taggers.

How to play

If tagged, freeze with one foot on the ball. A teammate unfreezes you by dribbling or passing through your legs.

Coaching cues

Look up, recognize teammates who need help, and control the ball before trying to rescue someone.

U6Let players simply dribble through the frozen player’s legs.
U7Require a controlled pass through the legs to unfreeze.
Treasure HuntTeamDribbling8–10 min
⌄
Setup

Put 20–30 cones/pinnies in the center. Give each team a “home” in a corner.

How to play

Players dribble out, collect one treasure, and dribble it home. Once the middle is empty, allow stealing from other teams.

Coaching cues

Control the ball while changing direction. Find the least crowded route.

U6No complicated rules; let the chaos be fun.
U7Add stealing and ask players to choose the safest open route.
KnockoutDribblingFree for all8 min
⌄
Setup

Everyone has a ball inside a grid.

How to play

Protect your ball while trying to knock other balls outside. If yours goes out: retrieve it, do 3 toe taps, and immediately return.

Coaching cues

Body between defender and ball. Keep your ball close before attacking someone else’s.

U6Use a larger grid and no elimination.
U7Shrink the grid as they improve to create more pressure.
Clean Your RoomShootingTeam8–10 min
⌄
Setup

Split the field in half. Scatter many balls on both sides.

How to play

Teams kick balls into the opponent’s half. After 45–60 seconds, stop. The side with fewer balls wins.

Coaching cues

Plant foot beside the ball, strike through it, and find open targets instead of kicking into a crowd.

U6Focus on lots of fun striking repetitions.
U7Add target zones or require passes below knee height.
1v1 to Two Goals1v1Attacking10 min
⌄
Setup

Create several tiny fields with one small goal on each end. Pair players by field.

How to play

Serve a ball in. Players compete 1v1 until someone scores or the ball leaves the field. Keep rounds around 20–30 seconds.

Coaching cues

Attack immediately. Get between defender and ball. If blocked, change direction.

U6Minimal instruction: “Go score!”
U7Encourage a fake, acceleration, or deliberate change of direction.
1v1 Escape Gates1v1Dribbling8–10 min
⌄
Setup

Put two small gates on opposite sides of a small grid. One attacker, one defender.

How to play

The attacker scores by dribbling under control through either gate. Defender tries to win the ball.

Coaching cues

If the defender takes away one gate, attack the other. Sell the fake before changing direction.

U6Make gates wide and defender pressure moderate.
U7Narrow gates or award bonus points for beating the defender after a fake.
1v1 to Goal1v1Shooting10 min
⌄
Setup

One goal. Attacker and defender start beside the coach or on opposite cones.

How to play

Coach serves the ball into space. Both players race to it and play live to goal.

Coaching cues

Win the ball, get your first touch toward goal, and shoot when space opens.

U6Give the attacker a small head start if needed.
U7Vary the service so players must react and transition quickly.
2v1 to GoalPassing2v110 min
⌄
Setup

Two attackers start together against one defender, going to goal.

How to play

Attackers can dribble or pass. Let the defender’s position create the decision.

Coaching cues

“If the defender comes to you, who might be open?” Do not force the pass if the dribbler has space.

U6Optional. Use only if the group is ready.
U7Great introduction to the dribble-or-pass decision.
2v2 Wave2v2Transition10 min
⌄
Setup

Two goals facing each other. Two attackers play against two defenders.

How to play

When a play ends, the defending pair leaves and two new players immediately attack the opposite direction.

Coaching cues

React quickly when possession changes. Attack before the defense gets organized.

U6Can work with a strong group, but keep rules loose.
U7Excellent for transition, support, and fast decisions.
Numbers Game1v1–3v3Competitive10 min
⌄
Setup

Two teams beside the coach. Give players matching numbers or simply call how many players enter.

How to play

Call “one” for 1v1, “two” for 2v2, or “three” for 3v3, then roll a ball in. Play until goal/out.

Coaching cues

Attack quickly, defend immediately, and recognize when a teammate creates a passing option.

U6Mostly call 1 or 2.
U7Mix 1v1, 2v2, and 3v3 frequently.
Partner Passing GatesPassingPartner8–10 min
⌄
Setup

Scatter cone gates. Pairs share one ball.

How to play

A pair scores when one player passes through a gate to the other. Try to score through five different gates.

Coaching cues

Move after passing. Find a new gate instead of standing still. Pass with enough pace to reach your teammate.

U6Allow dribbling between passes and use wide gates.
U7Encourage receiving across the body and moving immediately to the next gate.
Dribble & ShootShootingSolo8 min
⌄
Setup

Set up several small goals or cone goals around the area. Everyone has a ball.

How to play

Players dribble freely and shoot into any open goal. After scoring, they must find a different goal.

Coaching cues

Look up before shooting. Get the ball out of your feet before striking.

U6Keep goals close so success is frequent.
U7Require a change of direction or weak-foot finish occasionally.
3v3 / 4v4 ScrimmageGameTeam15–20 min
⌄
Setup

Small field, two goals, ideally 3v3 or 4v4 so everyone stays involved.

How to play

Mostly normal soccer. Keep coach stoppages rare and brief.

Coaching cues

Use questions rather than lectures: “Where is the space?” “Can anyone help the player with the ball?” Then restart immediately.

U6Mostly let them play. Celebrate brave dribbling and defending.
U7Occasionally highlight spacing, support, or a good pass without making passing mandatory.
Kids vs CoachesFinisherFun3–5 min
⌄
Setup

Kids attack one goal against the coaches, or play a very short mini-game.

How to play

Keep it playful and let the kids score. Use it as a high-energy ending rather than a teaching block.

Variation

Penalty kicks, crossbar challenge, or “score before coach steals it.”

U6Maximum silliness.
U7Still silly. They are six.
Dribble MazeWarmupDribbling8 min
⌄
Setup

Scatter cones randomly to create a “maze.” Every player has a ball.

How to play

Players dribble through the maze without touching cones or other players. Call out turns, stops, or speed changes.

Coaching cues

Small touches in traffic, bigger touches in space, and eyes up often enough to avoid collisions.

U6Just explore the maze and avoid obstacles.
U7Add inside/outside-foot challenges and quick changes of direction.
Body PartsWarmupBall control7 min
⌄
Setup

Everyone dribbles freely with a ball in a grid.

How to play

Coach calls a body part—foot, knee, elbow, head, bottom—and players stop the ball and touch that body part to it. Restart immediately.

Why it works

It mixes movement, stopping the ball, listening, and silliness without creating lines.

U6Keep it fast and funny.
U7Between calls, require a move such as pullback or outside-foot turn.
Hungry HipposDribblingTeam9 min
⌄
Setup

Put many balls in the middle. Split players into 2–4 teams with a “home” cone or square.

How to play

One player at a time from each team runs out, brings a ball home using their feet, then tags the next player. When the middle is empty, count the balls.

Progression

Have every player go at once, or allow stealing from another team’s home in the final round.

U6One at a time makes the game easy to understand.
U7All-at-once or stealing rounds add scanning and decisions.
Crocodile RiverDribblingVs9 min
⌄
Setup

Create a wide “river” with two cone lines. Players with balls start on one bank; 1–2 crocodiles begin inside.

How to play

Players cross the river while keeping their ball. Crocodiles try to knock balls away. Anyone who loses a ball retrieves it and rejoins next round.

Coaching cues

Look for a gap, change speed, and cut away from pressure rather than dribbling straight into it.

U6Make the river wide and keep only one crocodile initially.
U7Add safe islands that players may use for only two seconds.
Four-Goal Game2v2 / 3v3Decision making12 min
⌄
Setup

Small field with two cone goals on each end line, spaced apart. Play 2v2 or 3v3.

How to play

Each team can score in either of the two goals it attacks. No goalkeepers.

Coaching cues

If one goal is crowded, can you find the other? Encourage players to recognize and attack open space.

U6Use large goals and mostly let them discover the choice.
U7Ask quick questions about switching direction and supporting the ball.
End-Zone DribbleSmall-sidedDribbling10 min
⌄
Setup

Mark an end zone across each end of a small field. Play 2v2 or 3v3.

How to play

Score by dribbling the ball under control into the opponent’s end zone. No shooting from distance.

Why it works

It rewards beating a defender, keeping the ball close near pressure, and finding open lanes.

U6Use wide end zones and 2v2.
U7Play 3v3 and encourage support away from the ball.
Get Outta Here!1v1 / 2v2Fast transitions10 min
⌄
Setup

Two teams stand beside the coach, with a small goal at each end. Coach has a supply of balls.

How to play

Send in one or two players from each team and serve a ball. When the ball leaves or a goal is scored, shout “Get outta here!” and immediately send the next group.

Coaching cues

React quickly, attack immediately when you win it, and recover toward your goal when you lose it.

U6Mostly 1v1 with occasional 2v2.
U7Mix 1v1 and 2v2 so players constantly solve different problems.
Knock Down the CastleShootingPassing9 min
⌄
Setup

Build several “castles” from tall cones or stacked disc cones. Put players 6–10 yards away with balls.

How to play

Players pass or shoot to knock down a castle, retrieve their ball, rebuild it, and attack another target.

Coaching cues

Plant beside the ball and aim instead of just kicking hard.

U6Use big targets and short distances.
U7Make targets smaller or turn it into a team race.
Pass or Dribble GatesPassingDecision making10 min
⌄
Setup

Put several small gates inside a grid and play 2v2 or 3v3 with one ball.

How to play

A team scores by either dribbling through a gate or completing a pass through a gate to a teammate. The same gate cannot score twice in a row.

Coaching cues

Let the players choose: if space is open, dribble; if a teammate is open beyond pressure, pass.

U6Usually too complex; use regular Gates instead.
U7Good bridge between individual dribbling and team play.
Small-Sided World CupGame2v2–4v412 min
⌄
Setup

Create two or more tiny fields. Split into small teams and give each a fun country/team name.

How to play

Play 2–3 minute games, then rotate opponents. Keep score optional; the goal is lots of short games and touches.

Coaching cues

Keep rest short, substitutions minimal, and coaching mostly between rounds rather than during play.

U62v2 or 3v3 works best.
U73v3 or 4v4 with quick rotations.
